# Changelog

All notable implementation and documentation changes are recorded here.

## Unreleased

### Added
- **Service levels are enforced server-side (2026-11-12)** (`apps/backend/src/modules/sla/sla.service.ts`, new module; `chat.service.ts`, `dispute.service.ts`, `admin.routes.ts`). The Roadmap's helpdesk SLAs — "general support within 24 hours" and "disputes within 3 business days" — were previously a number drawn in the browser: the ops console computed "hours since the ticket was created" and coloured it. Nothing was enforced, nothing was stored, and nothing could be reported on.
  - **Two clocks per queue.** Support: first staff reply (24 h) and the ticket being closed (72 h). Disputes: the mediator answering (3 business days) and the dispute reaching `RESOLVED` (7 business days). The dispute first-response clock deliberately does not start at the raise: Stage 1 negotiation is designed to run seven days, so that reading would be breached by construction.
  - **Only the facts are stored** — `Conversation.firstRespondedAt`, `Conversation.resolvedAt`, `Dispute.firstRespondedAt` (migration `20261112140000_sla_tracking`). The deadlines are **derived** from `createdAt` plus the configured targets, so changing a target applies immediately to every open item and there is no stored deadline to go stale.
  - **Written once.** A later reply cannot rewrite the first response, and a late reply does not un-breach a clock that was already missed: a breach is a fact about what happened, not a snapshot of the present.
  - **Business days are Monday to Saturday**; Sunday is the only excluded day. Public holidays are not excluded — there is no holiday calendar in the system, and inventing one would silently drift from reality.
  - The state rides on `GET /api/v1/chat/admin/conversations` and `GET /api/v1/disputes/staff/queue`; `GET /api/v1/admin/sla/summary` counts the open queues by breach state. The ops console renders the server's state instead of recomputing it, and both queues can filter to breached cases.
  - **Not escalated.** A breach is visible, countable and filterable; nothing is automatically reassigned, escalated to a supervisor, or auto-closed. Enforcement that moves work between people without a human deciding is a larger decision than an MVP should make quietly.
  - Covered by `apps/backend/tests/sla-enforcement.test.ts`.

### Changed
- **CBE Birr and M-Pesa are handled manually (2026-11-12)** (`apps/frontend/src/BrowserWallet.tsx`): choosing either rail in the wallet now switches straight to the manual transfer flow with that rail's account details, a reference to quote and a receipt upload for staff verification. The previous behaviour offered a button that could only fail — the checkout was attempted, the backend refused it with `PROVIDER_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and the user had to interpret the failure before being offered the manual path. Both rails remain approved and both client stubs remain in place as the integration points.
- **The 7.5% take is all-in (2026-11-12)**: it covers the 7% platform commission, the payment/disbursement provider fee, and the cash-out charge the platform absorbs at payout rather than passing on to the freelancer. A freelancer who sees "92.5%" on one screen and then loses a further 0.5% at withdrawal was being charged twice for the same thing.
- **The launch-category list is ten, and ten is not a ceiling (2026-11-12)**: the enum grows by additive migration, so more subcategories can be added at any time without rewriting a single existing row.
- **A DPO is deferred (2026-11-12)**: the appointment happens in a later phase. The MVP launches without one and the founder holds the data-protection responsibility informally until then.
- **AI-assisted content is drafted, not published (2026-11-12)** (`docs/rule.md` §13.5): the assistant drafts, the founder reviews and signs off before anything reaches a user. Machine-translation artefacts in a shipped locale are a defect, not a cosmetic issue.

### Changed
- **Fee engine — the platform's overall take is now 7.5% (2026-11-12)** (`apps/backend/src/modules/escrow/fees.ts`, `apps/backend/src/config/env.ts`, `apps/backend/src/modules/finance/ledger.service.ts`): the payment/disbursement provider fee was a hardcoded `0.005` literal inside `withdrawFromWallet`, charged identically to every provider at payout time and never routed by the fee engine (`docs/hardening/AUDIT_FINANCE_WORKERS.md` finding L-3). It is now a configured component (`PAYMENT_PROVIDER_FEE_RATE = 0.005`), computed on the transaction gross at release and credited to `REVENUE-DISBURSEMENT-FEES`.
  - **7% platform commission** + **0.5% provider fee** = a **7.5%** overall take; the freelancer nets **92.5%**; the client pays no deposit surcharge.
  - `FeeBreakdown` gains `paymentProviderFeeMinor`. `recordMilestoneReleaseLedger` gains a third credit line so the double-entry journal stays balanced.
  - **The payout no longer deducts a cash-out fee**, so the platform carries Telebirr's own B2C charge out of its 7.5% instead of passing it on. Charging it in both places would have made the take 8%.
  - Frontend: `EscrowFeeBreakdown` shows the commission and the provider fee as two rows; `FeesPage.tsx` calculator, manifesto and comparison table updated to 7.5% / 92.5%; `DisputeQueue.tsx` and `EvidenceViewer.tsx` net figures updated; `i18n.ts` fee strings updated in all four languages.
- **Statutory withholding tax deferred and commented out (2026-11-12)** (`apps/backend/src/modules/escrow/fees.ts`): the 3% (with TIN) / 30% (without) deduction on payouts at or above ETB 10,000 is no longer applied. The ERA remittance channel, form numbers, filing frequency and responsible party are unconfirmed (`TAX-002`), and deducting an unverified statutory amount before the accountant confirms would take money from workers on a guess. The rates, the threshold and the `TIN_FEATURE_ENABLED` switch stay in `env.ts`, and the commented block is kept in place, so reactivation is a one-line change.
- **`docs/`**: the Phase 0 decision log, the 7.5% fee model, the deferred withholding, the ten launch categories and the M-Pesa approval are reflected in `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `COMPLIANCE_BLOCKERS.md`, `COMPLETENESS_CHECKLIST.md`, `DATABASE.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, `FLOW.md`, `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md`, `ROADMAP_ALIGNMENT.md`, `SYSTEM_DESIGN.md`, `WALKTHROUGH.md` and `DOCUMENT_UNIFICATION_2026-11-09.md`.
- **Master Design `PAY-003` superseded (2026-11-12)**: M-Pesa is an **approved collection rail** alongside Telebirr and CBE Birr. The M-Pesa client stub is deliberately **kept** — it is the integration point the decision keeps alive until Safaricom supplies the merchant API, the callback contract and credentials. `verifyWebhookSignature` still returns `false` and `initiatePayment` still throws, so no payment can be taken on that rail yet.
- **Auto-release window corrected to 7 days (2026-11-12)**: the local `apps/backend/.env` had `AUTO_RELEASE_WINDOW_DAYS=5`, contradicting both the code default and Service Agreement Clause 2.2(b). It is now `7`.
- **The native in-app support desk is the helpdesk (2026-11-12)**: no third-party helpdesk vendor. The Roadmap's SLA targets are **not** enforced in code — there is no SLA timer, no escalation and no auto-close.

### Security
- **Code-only compliance and defect pass (2026-11-10)** — the items from `docs/ROADMAP_ALIGNMENT.md` that needed no external decision, no counsel and no vendor. Six changes, each with its own tests.
  - **Two-factor approval on withdrawals** (`apps/backend/src/modules/escrow/withdrawalOtp.service.ts`, `apps/backend/src/modules/escrow/escrow.routes.ts`, `apps/backend/src/schemas.ts`, `packages/schemas/src/index.ts`). A withdrawal now requires an SMS code that approves **that** payout: `POST /api/v1/escrow/wallet/withdraw/otp` issues it, `POST /api/v1/escrow/wallet/withdraw` requires it as `otpCode`. The approval is consumed when the payout completes, so one code cannot authorise two payouts, and it is verified inside the service immediately before the wallet is debited, so no path to the money skips it. Business-rule refusals (active contract, open dispute, non-zero balance) are reported **before** the code is needed, so a blocked withdrawal never sends an SMS for a payout that was never going to happen. `PhoneVerificationCode` gains a `purpose` column (migration `20261110130000_phone_code_purpose`) so there is one outstanding code per (phone, purpose): a phone-verification code cannot cancel or substitute for a withdrawal code, which would otherwise have been a denial of service on a security control reachable by anyone who knows the number. The frontend (`BrowserWallet.tsx`) sends the code and shows a six-digit input; the offline mock mirrors the flow. The styles for this UI (`.withdraw-otp-actions`, `.withdraw-otp-dev`) were already in `index.css` — added by `371ed5c` as an orphaned hunk with no component using them, so this completes a half-landed change rather than introducing a new one.
  - **PDPP data-subject rights** (`apps/backend/src/modules/privacy/privacy.service.ts`, new module). `GET /api/v1/users/me/export` now returns the complete archive — account, profile, verification, financials, marketplace activity, communications and the compliance records — rather than an account summary with counters, and it reads the authoritative `KycRecord` instead of the denormalised cache that made a verified user read as `PENDING`. `POST /api/v1/users/me/erasure` destroys the name, bio, avatar, email, password, federated identifiers, chat and DM bodies, the portfolio, the skills, the marketplace copy, the notifications and the analytics; deletes chat attachments from the object store **and** its local-disk fallback, because nulling the row does not destroy the bytes; revokes marketing and analytics consent by appending a `granted: false` row rather than deleting the history; and closes the account (suspended, sessions revoked, credentials invalidated). The AML and financial records are retained, and the response lists exactly what was kept and why. Erasure is refused with `409 ERASURE_BLOCKED` and a specific list while money is in flight, a payout is pending, a contract is active or a dispute is open.
  - **10-year audit retention** (migration `20261110120000_audit_log_retention`). `AuditLog.retentionUntil` is NOT NULL and stamped at write time as `createdAt + AUDIT_RETENTION_YEARS` (default 10). The daily `audit-purge` worker calls the `SECURITY DEFINER` function `purge_expired_audit_logs()`, which the application role may execute without holding `DELETE` on the table — the "the app cannot delete audit rows" invariant survives. The function deletes a row only when **both** its stamp has expired **and** it is at least ten years old, so a misconfigured retention period cannot shorten a statutory obligation. The purge is itself audited.
  - **Breach-notification response chain** (`apps/backend/src/lib/breachAlert.ts`, `apps/backend/src/modules/admin/admin.routes.ts`, `apps/backend/src/modules/admin/permissions.ts`). The module was log-only with the dispatch commented out. It now contains the incident (revoking every affected user's sessions by bumping `sessionVersion`, without touching the account or its financial records), notifies staff in-app/SMS/Telegram with the deadline in the message, notifies each affected user individually, records the incident in the audit log, starts the 72-hour clock, and produces an ECA notification template with the PDPP-required fields filled in and the human-only fields marked. Staff open incidents through `POST /api/v1/admin/incidents/breach` (new `incidents.manage` permission) and list them with `GET /api/v1/admin/incidents/breaches`.
  - **`deleteObject()` in `apps/backend/src/lib/objectStore.ts`** — deletes the object *and* the local-disk fallback `uploadChatAttachment` writes, and reports whether the delete actually took effect, so an erasure in a bucket with object lock configured is visible rather than silent.
  - **`scripts/regenerate-prisma-client.ps1`** — `prisma generate` fails on Windows with `EPERM` **while the backend server is running**, because the running Node process has the query engine DLL mapped and Windows will not let a file that is open be replaced. The symptom misleads: the file can be *renamed* but not *deleted*, which looks like an ACL problem rather than a lock. Stopping the server makes `prisma generate` work again. This script covers the case where the server cannot be stopped — it generates into a scratch directory and copies everything except the engine across, which is all a schema edit actually changes.

### Fixed
- **The withdrawal-approval endpoint answered 500 when the SMS gateway was down** — `POST /api/v1/escrow/wallet/withdraw/otp` mapped only its own error types, so the SMS provider's failures fell through to the generic handler. The caller was told the platform had broken when the gateway had not, and a retry looked like a server fault rather than a transient one. It now answers **503 `SMS_UNAVAILABLE`** when a code cannot be delivered and **429 `RATE_LIMITED`** when the number is being spammed, mirroring the phone-verification route. Covered by a new regression test in `tests/withdrawal-2fa.test.ts`.
- **The browser-platform sidebar scrolled away with the body content** — `.platform-shell.sh-shell` is a grid, so the sidebar was a grid item and the grid stretched it to the height of the main content. On a long page it became taller than the viewport and scrolled off with the body, taking the navigation with it: by the bottom of the page there was no way to change section. The base rule now sets `position: sticky`, `align-self: start`, `height: 100vh` and `overflow-y: auto`, so the sidebar is pinned to the viewport and scrolls its own contents if the nav is taller than the screen. The `sh-variant-inset` and `sh-variant-floating` variants already declared a `top` and a viewport-relative `height` — those were inert without `position: sticky` and now do what they were written to do. The topbar was already frozen, and the ops-console sidebar was already `position: fixed`; the browser platform was the only one that scrolled.
  - **Verified, not assumed.** `scripts/verify-sidebar-fixed.mjs` drives headless Chrome over the DevTools Protocol (Node's built-in WebSocket — no browser-automation dependency) and measures the sidebar's viewport-relative position before and after scrolling the page. With the old CSS the sidebar's top went from `10` to `-1224` — entirely out of view. With the fix it stays at `10` and every check passes.
- **The canonical design document was wrong about the money path** — `docs/SYSTEM_DESIGN.md` stated that Chapa was a collection rail (it was removed in PAY-002), that the provider payout API was "not yet wired to live SDKs" (Telebirr B2C `initiateDisbursement()` is implemented and is what the withdrawal path calls), that the freelancer's net was 93% (it is **92.5%** — 7% platform fee **plus** the 0.5% provider fee, so the platform's take is a single 7.5%), and that withdrawal required Tier 2 KYC (under the MVP policy `TIN_FEATURE_ENABLED=false`, so it is Tier 1). All corrected, along with the route table (the `/api/v1/webhooks/chapa` route does not exist), the integration-stubs matrix, the withdrawal sequence diagram, and the KYC tier descriptions. The `PaymentProvider` enum is shown as it really is — `CHAPA` and `M_PESA` are inert members with no adapter — with a note explaining why they were not dropped.
- **The worker registry listed five queues; there are ten.** The `auto-release` row also described a 6-day warn and a day-5 release, which no longer matches the 24-hour warning and 7-day window in the code. Both corrected, and the audit-retention worker added.
- **The audit-log section did not mention the retention layer.** It now documents the NOT NULL `retentionUntil` stamp, the `SECURITY DEFINER` purge that leaves the application role without `DELETE` on the table, the hard ten-year floor in SQL, and the fact that the purge audits itself.
- **The frontend build section claimed React 18.** The project runs React 19 (`apps/frontend/package.json` pins `^19.3.0`); corrected, along with the stale "Amharic + English string catalog" note, which is now four locales.
- **A cross-file cleanup collision in the test suite** — `tests/critical-fixes.test.ts` deleted every ledger entry and journal whose idempotency key contained `"escrow-fund-"`, which is the **production** key prefix (`escrow-fund-${milestoneId}`) used by every suite that funds an escrow. Running in parallel against one shared test database, its `afterEach` therefore deleted other suites' funding journals, and those suites' escrow-pool assertions read a pool that had never been funded. The symptom was an intermittent failure in a *different* file (`dispute-partial-split-ledger.test.ts`, "the pool must be relieved exactly once"), which is the hardest kind of failure to attribute. The cleanup is now scoped to this file's own milestones, the way `escrow-wallet-funding-semantics.test.ts` already documented that it should be.
- **The armed cooling-off right (`ESC-002`)** — `createContractFromProposal()` never wrote `Contract.cancellableUntil`, although the schema documented that it would. `cancelDuringCoolingOff()` treats a missing value as "expired", so the 7-day cooling-off right the Terms of Service promise the client was never actually available — the whole code path was unreachable. It is now written as `createdAt + COOLING_OFF_DAYS` (env, default 7), and the auto-release guard in `autoRelease.handler.ts` can finally fire. The unused `COOLING_OFF_HOURS` (default `24`) has been deleted: two variables for one concept, one of them unused, is how the gap survived in the first place.
- **Stale fee copy across the user-facing surfaces** — the marketing pages and the fiscal receipts disagreed with the backend in three separate ways, and each was wrong in a way a user would be charged against: the withholding was shown as "2%" instead of the statutory **3% / 30%** above ETB 10,000; the FAQ still advertised a **2.5% client / 2.5% freelancer** split that the platform stopped charging in favour of **0% / 7%**; and `FiscalDocumentModal` defaulted to **2.5% / 2.5%**, so every fiscal receipt the platform generated showed fees it does not collect. All three now read from the same numbers the fee engine uses.
- **Dead code removed** — `apps/backend/src/middleware/requireKycVerified.ts` had zero importers and duplicated the live guard in `hardBlock.ts`, which additionally returns a machine-readable `nextAction` so the frontend can offer the next step instead of rendering a dead end.
- **The fee-engine change shipped without its tests** — `7babd4b` moved the provider fee into the fee engine and started collecting it at release, but the dispute and payout suites still asserted the old amounts, so 19 tests failed at `HEAD`. The behaviour is intentional (a single 7.5% take instead of 7% at release plus a surprise 0.5% at cash-out), so the **tests** were updated to the new arithmetic rather than the code reverted.
- **The committed route baseline was stale** — three live routes were missing from `docs/hardening/route-baseline.json`, so the route-inventory gate was failing at `HEAD`. Regenerated (191 routes).
- **The roadmap summary counts did not match the roadmap's own tables** — `docs/ROADMAP_ALIGNMENT.md` claimed 8 not-started items in Phase 2 where the table has 5, 3 blocked items in Phase 1 (backend) where the table has **none**, and 2 stub items in Phase 0 where the table has none. The counts are now taken from the tables, and `scripts/check-doc-parity.mjs` gained a check that fails CI when the two disagree, so the summary cannot drift again. `scripts/roadmap-alignment-counts.mjs` prints the counts and `scripts/roadmap-alignment-rows.mjs` lists every row with its status.

### Fixed
- **Flaky escrow auto-release timing assertion — fixed at the source (2026-11-12)** (`apps/backend/src/modules/escrow/escrow.service.ts`, `apps/backend/src/modules/gigs/gig.service.ts`, `apps/backend/tests/escrow-wallet-funding-semantics.test.ts`): `tests/escrow-wallet-funding-semantics.test.ts` asserts that the 24-hour warning is scheduled *exactly* 24 h before the auto-release (`releaseDelay - warnDelay === DAY`). The release deadline stays deliberately **calendar-day** arithmetic, because the Service Agreement says "seven calendar days"; but the warning lead is now always exactly 24 hours of elapsed time, even on a host that observes daylight saving.

  - The test's release-window upper bound was widened by one hour so that a DST transition (where seven calendar days is 167 or 169 hours rather than 168) cannot break it. The 24-hour gap assertion was left exact.
